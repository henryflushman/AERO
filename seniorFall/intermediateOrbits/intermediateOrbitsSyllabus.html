Course Description

Catalog Speak: Relative orbital motion and Rendezvous, Linearization of the equations of motion. Clohessy-Wiltshire equations. Two-impulse Rendezvous. Aspherocity of the earth, aerodynamic drag, solar radiation pressure, and n-body perturbations on an orbit. Encke, Cowell, and Variation of Parameters solution techniques. Specialty orbit due to perturbations such as low thrust trajectories, sun-synchronous, and molynia orbits.

Dr. A Speak: We are going to work through the different parts of formation flight and rendezvous and then the various types of integration methods for perturbations

Academic Honesty

While I encourage collaboration between students in order to understand the course material, the actual assignments MUST be done by each student independently. Inappropriate collaboration (also known as cheating) includes:

  • Using all or parts of assignments, exams or projects from this year or any previous year (even your own)
  • Talking, passing information or using inappropriate materials during an exam

The ultimate goal is for everyone to learn the material. Collaboration is an effective method for peer learning, but at some point in this course, each of you will be tested individually. In this manner, I will be assessing your individual mastery of the course material.

Anyone found to be participating in inappropriate collaboration may be immediately failed from the course. It is campus policy that any instance of cheating must be reported the university. Here is the University’s exact definition:   ”Cheating is defined as obtaining or attempting to obtain, or aiding another to obtain credit for work, or any improvement in evaluation of performance, by any dishonest or deceptive means. Cheating includes, but is not limited to: lying, copying from another’s test or examination; discussion of answers or questions on an examination or test, unless such discussion is specifically authorized by the instructor; taking or receiving copies of an exam without the permission of the instructor; using or displaying notes, ”cheat sheets,” or other information devices inappropriate to the prescribed test conditions; allowing someone other than the officially enrolled student to represent same.” See the Office of Student Rights and Responsibilities, OSRR Link and the “Campus Administrative Manual” (CAM), Section 684 for more information.

It's important for you to know the rules, and to know your rights. Please take a moment to review Cal Poly Academic Integrity Policy
